Install it in about 30 seconds

Shotglow isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet, so you load it yourself. It's the same "Load unpacked" flow developers use every day, and it takes four clicks.

  1. Download and unzip

    Use the button above. You'll get shotglow.zip — unzip it and keep the shotglow folder somewhere permanent, like your Documents folder. Chrome loads the extension from that folder every time it starts, so don't delete it.

  2. Open the extensions page

    Paste chrome://extensions into your address bar and hit Enter.

  3. Turn on Developer mode

    It's the toggle in the top-right corner of that page.

  4. Click "Load unpacked" and pick the folder

    Select the unzipped shotglow folder — the one containing manifest.json. Shotglow now appears in your toolbar and in the right-click menu on any image.

Updating later: download the newest zip, unzip it over the old folder, then click the refresh icon next to Shotglow on chrome://extensions.

What it does

Redaction is the point, but a screenshot you're about to post deserves to look good too.

Mosaic redaction

Drag boxes over anything sensitive. Four strength levels, from lightly scrambled to unrecoverable. The pixels are actually destroyed, not covered.

Frame and shadow

Margin, image scale, corner roundness and a drop shadow — the same treatment that makes a screenshot look deliberate instead of dashed off.

Backgrounds

Solid colors, eight gradient presets or a custom two-stop gradient, plus bundled mesh wallpapers. Add a dot, grid, diagonal or crosshatch overlay on top.

Aspect ratios

Export at Auto, 1:1, 4:3 or 16:9. The frame expands to fit the ratio — your image is never cropped.

Copy or download

One click puts the finished PNG on your clipboard, ready to paste into Slack, a doc or an issue. Or save it to disk.

Stays on your machine

Everything runs locally in the browser. No account, no upload, no server — there's nowhere for your screenshot to go.
